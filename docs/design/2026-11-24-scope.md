# Scope of erg, and the split into separate repositories

Date: 2026-11-24. Issue: #1. Decided by the maintainer; this record was written with an AI assistant.

## Decision

erg compares two ways of controlling the same robot:

- **split control**: one small controller per leg. Each controller sees only its own leg's sensors and
  the values exchanged with neighbouring legs, following the
  [Physical-Edge Native Architecture](https://physical-edge-native.github.io/) (regions that exchange
  only values on their borders);
- **central control**: one policy that sees the whole body.

The comparison is made **on a cheap four-legged robot, without vision**, and the goal is to **build the
robot from cheap devices and make it walk**. One aim is to find how far the cost of training, running and
building a controlled robot can be lowered.

erg makes no claim that split control is better than central control. The measured differences, including
where split control is worse, are the result.

## What erg covers

- the design of a four-legged robot that can be built from hobby servos and ESP32-class microcontrollers;
- sensing without cameras (joint angles, IMU, foot contact, possibly range sensors);
- simulation in MuJoCo on CPUs, with the robot description kept loadable by Isaac Lab for later use;
- training of the central policy and of the per-leg controllers on CPUs;
- running the per-leg controllers on the built robot;
- cost as a measured quantity: parts cost, CPU hours to train, computation per control step.

## What erg does not cover, and where it goes

| Topic | Where | Why not in erg |
|---|---|---|
| Split versus central control as a pure question of model architecture, at the size of industrial robots | [gauss](https://github.com/physical-edge-native/gauss) | Differences that matter at industrial size may not show on a small, cheap robot, so erg alone would not be a fair comparison of the architectures. |
| Physical reservoir computing: a soft body used as part of the computation | [poise](https://github.com/physical-edge-native/poise) | A soft body and a servo-driven body are different robots, and a physical component cannot be checked bit for bit, so the comparison in erg would no longer be on equal terms. |
| Robot control that takes a physical field (temperature, shape, airflow) as input | erg's future direction; a separate repository only if needed | |

Fields are observed with Dyne as it is: either sensors on the robot keep building a twin of its surroundings,
or sensors scattered in the environment build a twin that is fed to the control model.

## Use of Dyne

erg depends on [Dyne](https://github.com/physical-edge-native/dyne) wherever it can, instead of
re-implementing the same parts:

- the per-leg controllers are Dyne WebAssembly solvers (`mlp-core`, `mlp-actor`): trained in Python,
  exported through ONNX, and run with the same bits on a PC and on a microcontroller;
- messages between legs use Dyne's wire format (`dyne-wire`);
- message loss and delay in simulation come from Dyne's seeded virtual network (`dyne-core`);
- a leg that stops and comes back follows Dyne's rules for leaving and rejoining.

Dyne is not published on crates.io, so erg pins it as a git dependency at a fixed commit.

## Other options considered

- **Compare only the architectures, without cost or hardware.** Faster to start, but it would leave out
  one of the aims (lowering cost), and a small simulated robot would still not be a fair architecture
  comparison.
- **Keep all topics (including physical reservoirs) in erg.** One place for everything, but two different
  bodies and two ways of checking results would make both shallow.
