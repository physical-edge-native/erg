# Print-ready parts for one leg of body L1 (test print)

Date: 2026-11-27. Issue: #72 (builds on the layout CAD of #59, `erg/cad_l1.py`). This record was written with an AI
assistant. Code: `erg/cad_leg.py`; export: `scripts/export_leg.py`; tests: `tests/test_cad_leg.py`.

## Why

The layout CAD fixes where every part of body L1 is, but has no screw holes, pins or rod ends. The printer is available
before the parts arrive, so one leg (front left) and the cradle of its abduction servo are drawn print-ready first and
printed for a test fit. What the test print shows goes back into the parameters before the other legs and the torso
are printed.

### Hardware for one leg (to be checked against what comes with the servos)

- 12 M2 countersunk self-tapping screws for the end caps: 10 × M2 × 6, 2 × M2 × 10 (the knee cap's bottom pair goes
  through the thigh's web and plate);
- 12 M2.5 countersunk screws for the horns (shoulder 8–10 mm, thigh 6 mm, crank 5 mm), or the servo's own horn screws
  if they are of this kind;
- knee: one M3 × 18 low-head screw (DIN 7984), one M3 nut, one 3 × 5 × 5 mm sintered bronze sleeve;
- push rod: one carbon-fibre tube 4 × 3 mm, cut to 59 mm; two M2 ball links with their balls; the balls are held
  by M2 screws through the ball and the crank into a nut in the crank's pocket (the crank's boss is 9 mm and the
  shank's 7 mm, longer than the thread of most one-piece ball studs): about M2 × 16 on the crank, M2 × 14 on the
  shank; two M2 threaded studs (or set screws) about 14 mm long, glued 8 mm into the tube with epoxy;
- foot: one M2 × 25 screw with nut, one SS-5GLD, one rubber cap for a 24 mm sphere.

## Decided by the maintainer (2026-11-27)

After the first draft (findings below), the maintainer chose for the push rod a **longer crank (18 mm instead of
12 mm, both ends of the parallelogram)** and a **carbon-fibre tube** instead of the printed PLA rod, and, for the knee
servo's plugs, to **check on the real servo first** (nothing changed now). A firmware limit on the knee torque near
the ends of its range followed as Issue #81 (below).

### Push rod: forces and buckling

The parallelogram passes the knee servo's torque on one to one; the rod's force is torque / (crank · |sin q|) at
knee angle q (`cad_leg.rod_force`). At the servo's stall torque (2.94 N·m) with an 18 mm crank:

| Knee angle q (rad) | When | Rod force (N) |
|---|---|---|
| −1.54 | standing | 163 |
| −2.52 | folded, start of the stand-up sequence (most bent reached) | 280 |
| −0.44 | push of the stand-up sequence (straightest reached) | 383 |
| −2.6 | knee limit, bent | 317 |
| −0.2 | knee limit, straight | 822 |

The tube (assumed 4 mm outside, 3 mm inside diameter; to be checked on the tube bought) buckles (Euler, pinned at both
ball centres, length 77 mm, second moment of area π/64 · (4⁴ − 3⁴) = 8.6 mm⁴) at **1430 N** with E = 100 GPa, the low
end of 100–130 GPa for pultruded carbon tubes (1860 N at 130 GPa). With a **safety factor of 2** the allowable load is
**715 N**: the margin is 4.4 standing, 1.9 at the stand-up push, 2.6 when folded. Only the last part of the range
before the straight knee limit, **q from −0.23 to −0.2 rad**, exceeds it at stall (822 N, a factor of 1.74 on
buckling). The tube's compression strength (about 5 kN) is not the limit; the M2 ball links' rating is still unknown
and is to be checked when they arrive. `tests/test_cad_leg.py` checks that the force at stall stays under the
allowable load at standing and at every knee angle the stand-up sequence reaches.

### Knee torque limit (Issue #81, option C)

The leg firmware lowers the knee servo's torque limit near the straight end of the knee range, and the simulation's
servo model (`erg/servo.py`) applies the same limit, so training sees it. The rule is written once, in
`erg/knee_limit.py`:

    torque_limit(q) = min(stall, ALLOWABLE · CRANK · |sin(q + CRANK_PHASE)|)

with ALLOWABLE = 715 N (buckling load 1430 N / 2), CRANK = 0.018 m, CRANK_PHASE = 0 and stall = 2.94 N·m, so
ALLOWABLE · CRANK = 12.87 N·m. It is below the stall torque only for q from −0.2304 rad to the limit −0.2 rad,
where it falls to 2.56 N·m (87 % of stall); toward the bent end it would act only beyond −2.91 rad, outside the
range. The sine is `erg/exactmath.py`'s (`xmath.c` in the firmware), and the constants are generated into
`obs_layout.h` by `scripts/gen_firmware_layout.py`, so C and Python give the same bits (checked on a grid of knee
angles in `tests/test_firmware_host.py`).

- **Firmware** (`leg.c`, `main.c`): after reading the three servos, it computes the limit from the measured knee
  angle and writes the knee servo's torque limit register (address 48 = 0x30, RAM, two bytes, tenths of a percent of
  the servo's maximum torque, 0–1000, from Feetech's STS memory table; to be checked on the real servo). The value is
  rounded down, so the servo's limit is never above the rule's. It writes at once when the limit falls, and when it
  rises by 10 (1 %) or more or returns to 1000; otherwise it does not write. The write is one broadcast sync-write
  packet of 11 bytes, which the servos do not answer: 110 µs at 1 Mbit/s. It is sent once at start and then only
  while the knee is within about 0.03 rad of the straight limit, so walking and standing cost no bus time. The
  register's 100 % is taken as the stall torque (to be checked on the bench).
- **Simulation** (`erg/servo.py`): the knees' drive torque is clipped to the limit at the current knee angle,
  computed with the same function (unrounded, and without the firmware's 1 % step, which only lowers the servo's
  limit further). `ServoParams.position_actuator()` turns it off, so that it still reproduces the model's position
  actuators.
- **Checks** (`tests/test_knee_limit.py`): at the limited torque, and at the register's rounded-down torque, the
  rod's force stays under 715 N at every knee angle of the range (0.5 mrad grid). The stand-up sequence still gets
  up with the servo model and the limit from the back, either side and standing, with either push side. It does not
  reach the limited band (its straightest knee is −0.44 rad), so the limit does not change it.

## Decisions made in this step (small, within the issue)

- **Knee bearing**: an M3 screw from the outside through a bronze sleeve in the shank into a nut in the thigh's bar.
  The sleeve is 0.3 mm longer than the gap it sits in, so the screw clamps the sleeve and the shank turns on it.
  The screw's head sits in a counterbore so that nothing stands out of the shank's outer face.
- **Push rod plane**: the ball centres are 7 mm outside the leg's plane, outside the knee screw's head. Over the knee
  range (−2.6 to −0.2 rad) the rod passes as close as 9 mm to the knee pin (with the 18 mm crank), so in the plane of
  the knee hubs it would hit them; outside it, it clears everything (checked at both knee limits and at the angles of
  the stand-up sequence).
- **Tube ends**: the M2 threaded studs are glued straight into the tube's 3 mm bore with epoxy (0.5 mm of glue
  around the thread). A printed or aluminium insert in a 3 mm bore would have walls of 0.5 mm at most, so it adds
  nothing but a joint.
- **Foot as a separate part**: the sphere cannot be printed in one piece with the shank without supports. The foot is
  printed upside down on its flat top and pushed onto the shank's end. Its centre is in the leg's plane (y = 0), as in
  the MuJoCo model; the layout CAD had it 3 mm outside.
- **Thigh spacer as a separate part**, so that the thigh lies flat on its plate.
- **Shoulder without the layout's bridge**: the cap's back wall is inside the plate, and the layout's bridge reached
  1 mm into the hip flexion servo.
- **Countersunk screws everywhere**: some heads would otherwise stand into another part (the abduction horn's
  screws under the hip flexion servo's back face, the hip flexion horn's screws under the knee servo's connectors,
  the shoulder cap's outer screws 1.3 mm from the thigh's plate as it swings); one kind of screw for all caps.
- **Switch depth** (`SOLE_GAP` = 5.2 mm from the actuator face to the bottom of the sphere): keeps 2 mm of wall at the
  pocket's corners. The lever must reach through it (see the checklist).

## Findings of the first draft

The maintainer's choices on findings 1 and 2 are recorded above.

1. **The knee servo's cable connectors face the hip flexion horn.** In layout L1 the knee servo's bottom face (with
   the connectors, if they sit where the STS3215 model has them) is 3.5 mm from the thigh's plate, right over the hip
   flexion horn. If the plugs go in perpendicular to the face, there is no room for them. Options: (a) check on the
   real servo first (the plugs may enter from the side; the test print shows it); (b) turn the knee servo half a turn
   about its shaft, body pointing down the thigh (the connectors then lie below the horn, where the plate can have a
   window; the leg's inertia about the hip rises); (c) move the knee servo 6–8 mm outward (the leg becomes wider).
   Recommended: (a), then (b) if needed. **Chosen (2026-11-27): (a).**
2. **Push-rod force near the straight knee** (with the 12 mm crank of the first draft; chosen: an 18 mm crank and a
   carbon tube, with the firmware limit planned). With the parallelogram, the rod's force at the servo's stall torque
   (2.94 N·m) is 2.94 / (0.012 · |sin q|): 245 N standing (q = −1.54 rad), 420 N at q = −2.52 (folded while standing
   up), 575 N at q = −0.44 (the stand-up push), 1230 N at the knee limit q = −0.2. The printed rod buckles at about
   480 N (PLA, E ≈ 3.5 GPa, 77 mm). The ratings of the M2 ball links are not known yet. Options: limit the knee
   servo's torque near the ends of the range, narrow the knee range, a longer crank (the rod's force falls with the
   crank's radius; `CRANK` and `CRANK_PHASE` are parameters, and both cranks turn together, so the knee angle stays the
   servo's angle), or a stiffer rod (carbon tube). To decide after the test print and the ball links' rating.
3. **Layout corrections** for the next layout revision (no effect on the model): the torso's side walls through the
   abduction servos' ends and horns, the shoulder's bridge into the hip flexion servo, and the foot's centre 3 mm out of
   the leg's plane (all handled in `cad_leg.py`).

## Print settings (suggestion)

- PLA, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, 4 perimeters, 100 % infill (the masses above assume full infill; a first fit test
  can use 40 % gyroid, since the fits depend on the perimeters).
- No supports. Orientation as exported (`PRINT_ORIENTATION`; every part lies on a flat face): the cradle on its
  front face; the shoulder upside down on the top of its cap; the thigh, the crank and the shank on their inner faces;
  the foot upside down on its flat top. The largest unsupported spans are short bridges (the
  caps' roofs about 25 mm, the shoulder's window 20 mm, the switch pocket 13 mm).
- Elephant's foot compensation about 0.2 mm (the crank's and shank's nut pockets and the cradle's countersinks are on
  the bed). A brim for the thigh spacer.

## Test-print checklist (what to measure after printing)

1. Weigh each part; compare with the table (the 100 % infill print).
2. Holes with pins or drill bits: M2 clearance (2.5 drawn), M2.5 (3.0), M3 (3.5), the sleeve's bore (5.4 drawn: the
   sleeve should turn freely without play). Record how much smaller than drawn they came out.
3. Servo in each cap: slides in without force and without rattle (0.4 mm clearance); all four frame screws find the
   servo's threaded holes; the notch clears the raised plate; the countersunk heads are flush.
4. Horn patterns: the screws enter the horn's holes on the shoulder, thigh (with spacer) and crank; the centre hole
   clears the horn's centre screw.
5. The knee servo's connectors: can the plugs be inserted with the thigh in place (finding 1)?
6. Knee: the shank turns freely on the sleeve with the screw tight; axial play about 0.3 mm.
7. Push rod: measure the tube's diameters; set the ball links so that the knee angle equals the knee servo's angle; check at three angles (−0.5,
   −1.5, −2.5 rad) with a protractor; move the knee by hand over its range: nothing hits.
8. Foot: the switch slides into its pocket; with the foot unloaded the lever's tip stands out of the sphere; it clicks
   when the foot is pressed onto a table through the rubber cap; the cap stays on.
9. Move the whole leg by hand over the abduction and hip flexion ranges with the cradle held in a vice: nothing hits.
10. Write the measured values back into the parameters of `erg/cad_leg.py`.
