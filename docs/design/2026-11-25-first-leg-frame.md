# Frame of the first leg

Date: 2026-11-25. Issue: #25. Decided by the maintainer; this record was written with an AI assistant.

The frame is generated by [`erg/frame.py`](../../erg/frame.py) (build123d). `scripts/export_frame.py` writes STEP and
STL files for printing; the files are not kept in the repository. `tests/test_frame.py` checks that the
MuJoCo model has the frame's dimensions and masses and that no two parts overlap over the joint ranges.

## Decided (2026-11-25)

| Point | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Printer | A fused-filament 3D printer available to the maintainer (Bambu Lab X2D) | Became available on 2026-11-25; it replaces the outside printing service chosen earlier that day (PA12 nylon by multi jet fusion at JLC3DP). Parts can be printed and fitted one at a time, with no shipping |
| Material | PLA, 100 % infill | The only material at hand. The walls are 3-4 mm, so with four perimeters the parts are nearly solid anyway; full infill makes them stronger and their mass predictable. PETG (tougher) or nylon (tougher still) can replace PLA later without changing the shapes |
| Holes | Drawn 0.3 mm larger than the usual clearance (horn screws 3.0 mm, M2 frame screws 2.5 mm, M3 wood screws 3.7 mm) | Printed holes come out smaller than drawn |
| Servo dimensions | From the STS3215 CAD model of the SO-ARM100 project (Apache-2.0; `STEP/SO100/STS3215_03a.step`); only the measured values are used. **Checked on a real servo with calipers, and by a test fit of one printed part, before printing the whole leg** | The datasheet's drawing could not be read; this model is used by a widely built arm. Printing is cheap enough to start from it (maintainer's condition) |
| Layout | U-shaped brackets ("yokes"): each joint's bracket holds the servo's horn on one side and its idler on the other, and carries the next servo by an end cap screwed to that servo's top and bottom faces at its far end | Few parts; each joint is held on both sides, so it bends less than a one-sided plate. A push-rod knee (lighter leg) and a one-sided plate (simplest) were the other options |

## Resulting leg, and changes to the MuJoCo model

The model now follows the frame (it was an estimate before):

| | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Hip flexion shaft | 30 mm outward from the abduction shaft, at the same height | 61.2 mm **below** the abduction shaft, in the same vertical plane (the flexion servo hangs below the hip yoke) |
| Thigh, shank | 90 mm, 90 mm | unchanged |
| Hip, thigh, shank bodies | 80 g, 80 g, 30 g (estimates) | 82.1 g, 87.4 g, 45.4 g, with centres of mass and inertias computed from the parts (PLA, 1.24 g/cm^3, full infill) and the servos they carry |
| Knee range | -2.6 to -0.2 rad | -2.5 to -0.2 rad: at -2.6 the folded shank touches the thigh |
| Torso height at start | 0.17 m | 0.23 m, so that the standing posture starts just above the floor |
| Total mass | 1.51 kg | 1.61 kg (torso 0.75 kg, still an estimate) |

Printed parts of one leg (PLA): stand 22.7 g (end cap of the abduction servo with a plate for four M3 wood screws,
to fix the first leg on a board), hip 27.1 g, thigh 32.4 g, shank 45.4 g; 128 g of filament in all. Only the leg
(hip, thigh, shank: 105 g) belongs to the robot; the stand is for the bench.

How to print: each yoke lying on its side (the direction across the arms vertical), so that the layers run along
the arms and the turning load of the servo does not pull the layers apart; four perimeters, 100 % infill.

The thigh's arms sit outside the shank's (boss of 7.5 mm instead of 3 mm), so that the folded knee does not hit them.

## Known limits of this first version

- **PLA is brittle and creeps** (slowly deforms under a steady load): screws should not be over-tightened, and a
  part that cracks in a fall is reprinted (or printed in PETG once available). The weighed masses replace the
  computed ones in the model after printing.
- **Not yet measured**: the servo dimensions, the screw sizes of the horn (holes of 2.5 mm in the horn; clearance
  holes of 3.0 mm here) and of the case (pilot holes of 1.5 mm, taken as M2 self-tapping screws).
- **Foot switch not fitted**: its part number is not chosen (parts list). The shank ends in a solid block and a
  sphere; the block is the heaviest piece (45 g) and can be lightened once the switch pocket is designed.
- **One combination of joint angles collides**: with the knee fully folded (-2.5 rad), the hip cannot flex forward
  beyond -1.0 rad without the foot reaching the hip. Each joint's range alone is free. The MuJoCo model does not
  yet exclude this combination (parts of the robot do not touch each other in the model).
- **Right legs** are the mirror images of the left leg; their parts are to be generated mirrored before stage 2.
- The torso frame is not designed yet; on the bench, the stand takes its place.
